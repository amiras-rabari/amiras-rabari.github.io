About

From wiring cameras in data centers to writing a database from scratch.

I'm Amiras. I grew up in India and moved to Canada in May 2019 as an international student. I study Computer Science at Carleton University in Ottawa, in the AI and Machine Learning stream, and I work part-time on the IT team for Carleton Athletics.

Hardware first

I started at Seneca College in computer engineering and finished with a diploma in electronics engineering technology. That meant circuits, PCB design, microcontrollers and a lot of time with an oscilloscope. My capstone was a flood detection system on a board I designed myself.

After graduating, I worked as a security technician. I installed and networked hundreds of IP cameras and access control devices, including on a data center site. I loved the troubleshooting: working from the physical layer up until the problem had nowhere left to hide.

Then software

I always wanted to work closer to the code. While working full-time, I taught myself enough to pass CompTIA Security+ in 29 days. That showed me I could learn almost anything if I gave it real time. I went on to Java training at FDM Group, worked in technical support, and then started my Computer Science degree.

Now I build the things I used to only use. I wrote a relational database from scratch because I wanted to know what actually happens between a SQL query and the disk.

Why low-level

I've always wanted to understand how things work, and computers most of all. I enjoy working with the components themselves: opening a machine up, installing and swapping parts, and tracing a fault down to the piece of hardware that caused it.

That same curiosity is what pulled me into software. I read the RISC-V edition of Patterson and Hennessy to understand what the processor is actually doing underneath my code, and I like learning how chips are designed and made. Firmware and systems programming sit right where hardware meets code, which is where my background points. Long term, I want to work on large, real infrastructure: the hardware and systems software that everything else runs on.

Outside of code

I watch football (soccer) and play it on the PS5. I like educational YouTube, especially Veritasium, and I listen to an audiobook for fifteen or twenty minutes every night, usually philosophy or personal development.
