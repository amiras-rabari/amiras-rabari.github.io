About

From configuring network infrastructure at a data center to engineering a database engine from scratch.

I’m Amiras. I grew up in India and moved to Canada in 2019. Currently, I’m studying Computer Science at Carleton University in Ottawa (focusing on AI and Machine Learning) while working on the IT team for Carleton Athletics.

Systems & engineering roots

My engineering foundation started at Seneca College in Electronics Engineering Technology, focused on embedded systems, logic design, microcontrollers, and system diagnostics. For my capstone project, I engineered an automated flood detection system from the board up.

After graduating, I worked as a technician installing and networking IP security systems and access control devices for a data center client. What drove me most was system-level troubleshooting: isolating network and infrastructure failures layer by layer until the problem was resolved.

Transition to software engineering

Driven to build closer to software and core systems, I self-studied system security while working full-time and passed the CompTIA Security+ exam in 29 days—proving how fast I could master complex technical domains through deep focus. That led to software training at FDM Group, technical support engineering, and ultimately my Computer Science degree.

Now, I build the core systems that I used to only consume. Most recently, I wrote a relational database engine from scratch to understand the entire engineering pipeline between SQL execution and disk I/O.

Why systems & core infrastructure

I’ve always been driven by how complex systems work under the hood—whether analyzing machine architecture, diagnosing system failures, or optimizing performance at the hardware-software boundary.

Reading Patterson & Hennessy (RISC-V edition) reframed how I think about software execution, connecting high-level algorithms directly to underlying processor architecture. Systems programming, firmware, and database engine design sit right at the core where code meets hardware. Long term, I aim to engineer large-scale infrastructure: the low-level systems software and platform architecture that everything else runs on.

Outside of code

When I’m offline, I play football (soccer) on PS5, watch scientific deep-dives on YouTube (especially Veritasium), and unwind every night with 15–20 minutes of philosophy or personal development audiobooks.
